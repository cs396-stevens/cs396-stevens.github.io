    Course Description

    This course presents the basic concepts of computer security, the different vulnerabilities that can occur throughout a system, how malicious attackers exploit these vulnerabilities, the defenses that can prevent or mitigate an attack, and the consequences and costs of attacks to individuals, organizations and societies. Topics include the security of cryptographic schemes, system software, networks, databases and programs, as well as the ethical, legal, and regulatory considerations surrounding data privacy and security.

    Learning Objectives

    • (Cryptographic Systems) Compare and contrast private and public key cryptosystems, and identify the strengths and potential pitfalls of cryptographic systems. [Analysis]
    • (Systems Software) Illustrate and detect vulnerabilities in systems; explain how attacks can exploit such vulnerabilities; explain the defense mechanisms that can detect and prevent such attacks; and explain malware propagation mechanisms and defense strategies. [Development]
    • (Data privacy and security) Analyze access control mechanisms to restrict database access, and use cryptography to maintain the privacy of data stored in databases. [Professionalism]
    • (Network Security) Evaluate standard protocols to secure network communications and apply them in different scenarios; describe vulnerabilities in networking protocols that can be used to attack organizations; and understand how these can be detected or prevented. [Development]
    • (Programming for Security) Analyze how programs inadvertently create security vulnerabilities, and how these can be detected and prevented at the design, implementation, or deployment stage. [Development]
    • (Social Impact) Describe the implications of malicious attacks on computer systems to individuals, organizations, and society. [Professionalism]
    • (Ethics) Explain ethical issues in cybersecurity and state how the job of a typical IT professional might be connected to major technology-related ethical issues of the day. [Professionalism]

    Course Format and Structure

    Course attendance consists of:

    • 3 weekly ~1hr lectures (Mon, Wed, Fri)
    • 1 weekly ~1hr lab section (Thu)

    Attendance is required for both lectures and lab sessions.

    Lectures

    Lecture attendance is tracked using in-class poll exercises over the course of the semester. These are graded on completion, not correctness, graded as follows:

    def polls_score(
    num_answered: int, # number of answered polls over semester
    total_polls: int,  # total number of polls during semester
):
    percent_answered = num_answered / total_polls * 100
    if percent_answered >= 70:
      return 100
    else:
      return percent_answered

    In other words, completion of 70% of the polls will result in full credit for the semester.

    Labs

    We use pwn.college for our lab activities. More details about pwn.college will be included in the first lab.

    In general, the labs will consist of a series of increasingly difficult capture-the-flag (CTF) challenges related to the concepts covered in lecture. As part of lab completion, you will also submit a post-lab survey after completing the challenges. Challenges are meant to be completed within your scheduled lab section time, but may be completed any time before the post-lab deadline.

    The challenges take place in a Linux environment, and you are expected to get comfortable with it, as working in a Linux environment is an expected skill from prerequisite courses. If you find yourself struggling with the first few labs, we strongly suggest you try some of the other challenge modules available on pwn.college to get up to speed.

    Labs may be completed collaboratively, and with the help of AI, but all students are responsible for submitting solutions on pwn.college individually. Challenge completion is tracked on pwn.college.

    Labs are graded as follows:

    def lab_score(
    completed_challenges: int, # number of completed lab challenges
    total_challenges: int,     # number of lab challenges assigned
    survey_completed: int,     # 1 if post-lab survey completed, 0 otherwise
    attendance: int,           # 1 if attendance marked, 0 otherwise
):
    challenge_score = completed_challenges / total_challenges * 80
    return survey_completed * challenge_score + (attendance * 20)

    Other important details:

    • CAs will take attendance at every lab section (and note who leaves early).
    • Lab instructions will be released by Thursday 8am before lab sections.
    • Labs will be due by Friday 11:59pm the following day, including the post-lab.
    • There is a 1-hour grace period for submission.

    Extra Credit

    Additionally, it will be possible to earn extra credit through doing additional specific pwn.college challenges. The list of such challenges will be made available on the course website and may be updated throughout the semester.

    In general, easier challenges on the list will be worth fewer points. They are primarily there to incentivize you to take the time to build more experience in the challenge environment. Correspondingly, harder challenges worth more points are there as a reward for taking initiative and going deeper.

    The maximum amount of extra credit available through completion of these challenges will be capped. The instructor(s) reserves the right to increase (or decrease) that cap as they see fit.

    Homework

    There will be 5 problem sets assigned over the course of the semester that may include a mix of free response questions, short essays, and programming. These will be graded on correctness.

    • You may discuss these problem sets with one another, and with AI, but you may not directly share solutions or code. If you do discuss with other students or AI, you must list your collaborators on your submission. e.g.

      Collaborators:
- John Doe
- Jane Doe
- ChatGPT (GPT 5.6 Sol)
- Gemini 3.6 Flash

    • You may not use AI to complete the problem sets for you. If caught, you will at minimum receive a 0 on the assignment, and the incident reported to the Honor Board.

    • All students are still responsible for submitting their solutions individually on Gradescope.

    Late Policy

    Note: this policy applies only to homework.

    • You have 3 total slip days to use throughout the semester for homework extensions. Slip days are applied automatically. Do not email the instructor to use them.

    • Following any slip days used, there will be a 20% penalty for each additional day late. The last day to submit an assignment for a maximum 50% penalty will be explicitly stated on the the assignment instructions.

    • There is a 10 minute grace period for assignment submissions before slip days or penalties are applied.

    In the case of medical emergencies or exceptionally extenuating circumstances, please contact the instructor as soon as possible.

    Academic Integrity

    Generative AI

    You may use generative AI technology (e.g. GPT, Gemini, Claude, etc.) with care to generate ideas, brainstorm, and do research. However, you should note that the material generated by these programs may be inaccurate, incomplete, or otherwise problematic.

    You may treat these programs like a virtual fellow student: you are allowed to “discuss” with them at a conceptual level (though be aware that the information they provide may be inaccurate), but you cannot share solutions or code in either direction. Presenting AI-generated work as your own is plagiarism.

    If you include material generated by an AI program, it should be cited like any other reference material and clearly attributed with the model identifier. (e.g. openai/gpt-5.6-sol, openai/gpt-5-5, anthropic/claude-opus-4-8, anthropic/claude-fable-5, see docs.aimlapi.com for more examples)

    Any plagiarism or other form of cheating will be dealt with under relevant Stevens policies.

    Disability Services Confidentiality Policy

    Student Disability Files are kept separate from academic files and are stored in a secure location within the Office of Disability Services. The Family Educational Rights Privacy Act (FERPA, 20 U.S.C. 1232g; 34CFR, Part 99) regulates disclosure of disability documentation and records maintained by Stevens Disability Services. According to this act, prior written consent by the student is required before our Disability Services office may release disability documentation or records to anyone. An exception is made in unusual circumstances, such as the case of health and safety emergencies.
